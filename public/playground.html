CRDT playground

Three peers (Ann, Ben and Cy) share one document. Each has its own copy and its own Fugue CRDT, and they exchange operations over a simulated network. Type in any of the boxes, make the network slow, or take someone offline and edit on both sides. When everyone reconnects, every copy converges to the same text.

Inside the CRDT

The Fugue tree in 's copy. Reading the nodes left to right gives the text. Every character is a left or right child of another one. Deleted characters stay behind as faded tombstones, so edits made concurrently still have something to attach to. Ring colours show who typed each character.

Why not just send "insert 'x' at index 5"?

Indexes go stale. If Ben deletes the first word while Ann's "insert at 5" is still in flight, position 5 now means something else. A CRDT gives every character a permanent id, and expresses edits relative to ids instead of positions.

How Fugue orders characters

A new character becomes the right child of the character before it. If that one already has right children, the new character becomes the left child of the next character instead. The document is the tree's in-order traversal. Siblings are sorted by id, so every peer builds the same tree no matter what order operations arrive in.

Why words don't get shuffled

When you type a word, each letter is the right child of the one before it, so the word forms one branch. Two people typing at the same spot create two sibling branches, and an in-order traversal reads each branch whole. Try "Demo: typing at the same spot" above.

Offline and out of order

Each peer tracks a version vector: the last operation it has from every peer. On reconnect, peers swap version vectors and send exactly what the other side is missing. An operation that arrives before its parent character waits in a buffer (shown as "buffered" under each box).
